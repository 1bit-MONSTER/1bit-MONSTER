// the hard truth

22 libraries. 209 bitstreams. Zero docs.

AMD shipped the Ryzen AI Max+ 395 with a 50 TOPS XDNA 2 NPU and locked it behind a closed-source runtime: 22 proprietary .so files, 209 bitstreams, no documentation. Nothing outside AMD's own tooling could touch that chip.

We reverse-engineered the whole stack in 4 days and replaced it with open C++ — one MIT-licensed engine that runs LLMs on the NPU, on AMD / NVIDIA / Apple GPUs, or on plain CPU.

read the full journey
Every crash, breakthrough and bug, documented in real time.

What it started as

A laptop, a disassembler, and no documentation. The goal was narrow: get one model running on the NPU without the vendor runtime.

What it became

An inference engine covering five backends and 19 architecture classes, with a unified control plane, speculative decoding, image generation and a full local voice pipeline on top.

// why it is hard to copy
the NPU path exists at all

Recovered from a closed runtime and reimplemented in open C++. The engine is MAX-free by design; the max-xdna-backend repo stands as separate evidence that the chip can be driven from outside AMD tooling.

no python in the runtime

Most of the field is a Python stack over vendor kernels. That is a deployment ceiling this engine does not have: one auditable binary, embeddable where a CUDA container cannot go.

coverage compounds

Class-level support means each new architecture is cheaper than the last, and every one widens the catalog permanently — including checkpoints that do not exist yet.

one artifact

A single MIT-licensed engine, no runtime to install. Deployment is copying a file.
